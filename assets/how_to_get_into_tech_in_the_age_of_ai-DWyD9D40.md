# How to Get Into Tech in the Age of AI
*March 3, 2026*
*Jay*

AI is changing the tech industry fast. Job descriptions are shifting. New tools are appearing every week. Some people see all of this as a threat. Others see it as the biggest opportunity in a generation.

If you are trying to break into tech right now, here is how to make the current landscape work in your favor.

---

## The Landscape Has Changed

Five years ago, the standard advice was: learn to code, build a portfolio, get an internship, land a job. That path still works, but AI has added new dimensions.

Coding assistants like [GitHub Copilot](https://github.com/features/copilot) can generate boilerplate in seconds. AI tools can build entire prototypes from a prompt. Companies are hiring for roles that did not exist two years ago, like prompt engineers, AI safety researchers, and agent developers.

This does not mean traditional skills are irrelevant. It means the bar has shifted. Knowing how to code is still important, but knowing how to **think, build, and ship** alongside AI tools is what sets people apart.

---

## Learn by Building, Not Just Watching

Tutorials and courses are fine as a starting point, but they will not get you hired. Real learning happens when you build something and put it out there.

Pick a problem that interests you. Use AI tools to help you solve it. Ship it. It does not have to be perfect. A portfolio of three real projects will always beat a list of twelve certificates.

Some ideas to get started:

- Build a personal website or blog (like this one).
- Create a small tool that solves a problem you have.
- Build a chatbot or AI assistant using an open source model.
- Contribute a feature or fix to an open source project.
- Automate something tedious in your daily life with Python.

The project itself matters less than the fact that you shipped it and can talk about the decisions you made.

---

## Use AI as a Learning Accelerator

AI coding assistants are not replacing developers. They are making developers faster. If you are learning, use these tools to your advantage:

- **Explain code you do not understand.** Paste a function into Copilot or ChatGPT and ask it to break it down line by line.
- **Generate boilerplate.** Let AI handle the repetitive setup so you can focus on the interesting logic.
- **Explore new languages and frameworks.** Ask questions as you go instead of reading documentation cover to cover.
- **Debug faster.** Paste a stack trace and get an explanation of what went wrong and how to fix it.
- **Learn patterns.** Ask AI to show you different ways to solve the same problem and explain the tradeoffs.

The developers who learn to work alongside AI will move faster than those who avoid it. Treat it as a pair programming partner, not a replacement for understanding.

---

## Contribute to Open Source Early

You do not need years of experience to contribute to open source. Start with documentation improvements, bug reports, or small fixes. The barrier to entry is lower than most people think.

Why this matters for your career:

- It shows employers you can collaborate with other developers.
- It proves you can follow real-world engineering processes (pull requests, code reviews, CI/CD).
- It gives you public, verifiable work that lives on your GitHub profile.
- It connects you with communities where job referrals happen organically.

A strong open source contribution history often matters more to hiring managers than a polished resume with no public work to back it up.

---

## Pick a Niche, Not Just a Language

Instead of trying to learn everything, go deep on one area:

- **Cloud and infrastructure** (Azure, AWS, GCP, Terraform)
- **AI and machine learning** (Python, PyTorch, model fine-tuning)
- **Web development** (React, Next.js, full-stack applications)
- **DevOps and platform engineering** (CI/CD, Kubernetes, monitoring)
- **Security** (application security, identity, threat modeling)
- **Data engineering** (pipelines, SQL, analytics platforms)

AI is expanding every niche, not eliminating them. The people who understand a domain deeply and know how to apply AI tools within it will be the most in-demand. A generalist who knows a little about everything is less valuable than someone who knows cloud infrastructure deeply and can leverage AI to automate deployments.

---

## Build Your Network in Public

The tech community rewards people who share what they know. You do not need to be an expert to start writing or talking about what you are learning.

- **Write blog posts** about problems you solved or concepts you learned.
- **Share projects** on GitHub with clear READMEs.
- **Post on social media** about your learning journey.
- **Give talks** at local meetups or virtual events. You do not need a big audience to start.
- **Engage with communities** on Discord, GitHub Discussions, or tech Twitter.

One blog post explaining how you debugged a tricky issue can lead to a conversation that leads to a referral that leads to a job. It happens more often than you think. The people who get opportunities are usually the ones who are visible.

---

## Do Not Let Imposter Syndrome Stop You

Everyone in tech felt unqualified at some point. The senior engineers, the conference speakers, the people with impressive titles, all of them were beginners once. The difference is they started.

AI has leveled the playing field in many ways. You can build things today that would have required a team of engineers five years ago. You have access to the same models, the same tools, and the same documentation as everyone else.

The only real requirement is showing up and being willing to learn. You will feel uncomfortable. You will write bad code. You will submit pull requests that get rejected. That is the process. Every experienced developer went through it.

---

## A Practical Starting Plan

If you are starting from zero, here is a concrete path:

1. **Learn [Python](https://www.python.org/).** It is the most versatile language for AI, automation, web development, and data work.
2. **Build something small.** A script, a tool, a simple website. Ship it to [GitHub](https://github.com/).
3. **Learn [Git](https://git-scm.com/) and GitHub.** This is how every team collaborates. It is non-negotiable.
4. **Pick a direction.** Decide if you want to go toward web development, cloud, AI, data, or another area.
5. **Contribute to open source.** Find a project in your chosen area and make your first contribution.
6. **Use AI tools daily.** Get comfortable with Copilot, ChatGPT, or Claude as part of your workflow.
7. **Share what you learn.** Write about it, talk about it, put it out there.
8. **Apply broadly and follow up.** Your GitHub profile, blog, and contributions are your resume.

---

## Final Thought

AI is not closing the door to tech. It is opening it wider. The tools are more powerful, the resources are more accessible, and the opportunities are more diverse than at any point in history.

The question is not whether you are qualified. The question is whether you are willing to start.
